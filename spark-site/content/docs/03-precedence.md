Each of these projects does one part of what Spark does. None of them does all of it.

| Project | What it is | Like Spark | Different from Spark |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Project Genie** (Google DeepMind, 2026) | You describe a world, AI generates it, and you explore it in real time. | Works for any subject | Screen only, no physical controls |
| **Dataland** (Refik Anadol, Los Angeles, 2026) | An AI art museum filled with immersive generated visuals. | AI and immersive | You only watch, you don't create |
| **Exploratorium** (San Francisco) | Hands-on science exhibits designed to make visitors curious. | Physical play | One fixed topic, and you leave empty-handed |

# The gap Spark fills

Spark puts the three together: any subject, like Project Genie; an AI-made world, like Dataland; and hands-on physical play, like the Exploratorium. Then it adds what none of them has. You create inside your Spark and you keep it, so a first moment of curiosity can turn into something you actually pursue.

The two museums show the gap most clearly. In both, the museum makes the experience and the visitor consumes it. Spark turns that around, so the person curious about the subject is the one making it.
