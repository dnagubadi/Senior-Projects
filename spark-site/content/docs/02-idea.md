# The problem

People are sometimes afraid of, or put off by, pursuing something they're passionate about.

- Starting something new can feel risky, like you might fail or look silly.
- It's hard to know where to begin.
- So the curiosity fades before it turns into anything.

[[Add the moment or story that made you notice this problem.]]

> **The big question:** what if trying something you're curious about felt like play, with nothing to lose, and gave you a reason to keep going?

# Making a Spark takes five steps

1. **Ask:** say what you're curious about.
2. **Glow:** AI builds your Spark.
3. **Explore:** the Sparkplug's joystick, knobs and buttons move you through it.
4. **Create:** press Ignite to make something new.
5. **Keep:** save it to My Sparks, then edit it, build on it or share it.

# Example: Maya's Volcano Spark

Maya says "volcanoes." Her Volcano Spark forms on screen. She turns a knob to heat the magma and presses Ignite to erupt a new island. Then she saves it, opens it in Spark Studio, and adds a second volcano of her own.

# User flow

1. Open Spark.
2. Choose a subject.
3. AI builds your Spark.
4. Play with the controller.
5. **Try another subject?** Yes takes you back to step 2. No moves on.
6. **Save it?** Saved Sparks go to **My Sparks**.
7. From My Sparks you can share it to the library, edit it in **Spark Studio**, or build one from scratch.

# Where Spark lives

Spark works the same way wherever it's set up. The workflow above doesn't change, only the setting does.

- **At the spring expo:** a museum-style setup, with the Sparkplug and a screen set up as an exhibit people walk up to.
- **At home, in a classroom or anywhere else:** the same controller and the same digital interface, and the same five steps.

Wherever you make a Spark, it's saved to My Sparks, so you can pick it up again somewhere else.
